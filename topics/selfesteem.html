Low self-esteem

That loud inner voice — it isn't accurate.

Sometimes the cruelest person in your life isn't someone who hates you.

It's the voice inside your own head.

It knows all your weak spots.

Knows exactly what you're ashamed of.

Knows the mistakes you wish you could forget.

Knows the people you compare yourself to.

And it knows exactly how to pick the most painful moment to say: "See? I told you. You're not good enough."

You can get ten compliments and remember the one criticism.

Do a hundred things right and lie awake all night over the one mistake.

Look at someone else and see their best qualities — and look at yourself and see only flaws.

That's not objectivity.

That's a distorted way of seeing yourself.

And it's important to learn to tell the difference between:

"I feel this way" and "this is actually true."

If you feel unattractive, that doesn't mean you are.

If you feel like a failure, that doesn't mean you are one.

If today you think you can't do anything right, that doesn't mean it will always be this way.

Feelings are real.

But feelings don't always tell the truth.

Try, today, not to force yourself to love yourself.

That might be too big a task right now.

Try something simpler.

Stop tearing yourself down.

If you made a mistake, say:

"I made a mistake."

Not:

"I'm worthless."

If something didn't work out:

"That didn't work out."

Not:

"Nothing ever works out for me."

If someone rejected you:

"That relationship didn't work."

Not:

"I'm impossible to love."

One mistake doesn't describe an entire person.

One rejection doesn't define how lovable you are.

One failure doesn't define your abilities.

One bad stretch doesn't define your whole life.

And you don't have to be the best person in the room to have the right to be in it.

You don't have to be the prettiest.

The smartest.

The most successful.

The most confident.

You don't need to win a competition you never signed up for.

You have the right to take up space.

The right to speak.

The right to be wrong.

The right to learn.

The right to be imperfect.

The right to be yourself.

Please try, slowly, to turn your inner voice from an enemy into someone who's learning to be on your side.

You don't have to wake up tomorrow overflowing with self-love.

Start with something smaller: neutrality.

"I exist. I'm trying. Things are hard for me sometimes. I still deserve kindness."

Sometimes that's exactly where real change begins.

And if no one has ever said this to you clearly enough, I'll say it here:

you don't have to do something to become a person worth caring about.

You don't have to become prettier, more successful, smarter, more confident, or stronger first.

You're already a person.

And that's enough to deserve respect, care, and the chance to live your life.

If this feels bigger than a website can hold, that's okay — that's what Support, below, is for.
