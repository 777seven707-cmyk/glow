Depression

Everything feels flat, heavy, or far away.

If you feel like you don't want anything anymore, please hear one thing first:

you don't need to be ashamed that this is hard for you.

Depression can look nothing like it does in movies.

Sometimes it isn't tears.

Sometimes it's the complete absence of tears.

It can be a feeling of emptiness when you look at the world around you and realize that things that used to mean something to you now leave nothing happening inside.

You can wake up in the morning already tired.

You can lie in bed for a long time — not because you're lazy, but because even a simple action feels like it needs an enormous amount of energy.

You can look at messages and not have the energy to answer.

You can love people and, at the same time, want no one to touch you.

You can want things to change and, at the same time, not have the strength to take even the first step.

And maybe the most painful part — starting to hate yourself for it.

"Why am I like this?"

"Why can't I just pull myself together?"

"Other people manage."

"I'm not doing anything."

"I'm useless."

If you recognize yourself in these thoughts, please don't turn your state into a case against yourself.

A person who can barely breathe shouldn't have to prove they can breathe harder.

A person who is exhausted doesn't become stronger by calling themselves weak.

You might not feel well not because you're not trying hard enough.

Sometimes a person genuinely needs help.

And help is not defeat.

It doesn't mean you're weak.

It means you're in pain, and you deserve support.

If you managed to get out of bed today — that's already an action.

If you ate something — that matters.

If you went outside — that matters.

If you wrote to someone — that matters.

And if the only thing you managed today was to get through the day, that matters too.

Don't compare your hardest day to someone else's best photo.

Don't demand your old speed of yourself while you're fighting a battle no one else can see.

You don't have to become happy today.

You don't need to find the meaning of life right now.

You don't have to force yourself to feel joy.

Start by allowing yourself to simply exist, without constantly punishing yourself for not being who you'd like to be right now.

And please don't believe every thought that shows up in your darkest moments.

A depressive state can make the future feel empty.

It can create the feeling that nothing will ever change.

But feeling like the pain is permanent doesn't mean the pain will actually last forever.

A state can change.

Therapy can help.

Support can help.

Treatment can help.

Talking can help.

Sometimes recovery begins long before a person starts to feel happy again. It begins the moment they stop being alone with their pain.

If things feel bad enough that thoughts of death or self-harm are showing up, please don't stay alone. Call someone you trust, ask them to be with you, and reach out for urgent professional help.

And if right now you're just tired — very tired — please stay a little longer.

Don't decide your whole life today.

Today's task can be much smaller: just make it to tomorrow morning.

And that's enough.

If this feels bigger than a website can hold, that's okay — that's what Support, below, is for.
