Disability & chronic illness

The exhaustion of a world not built with you in mind.

There's an exhaustion that doesn't show.

People can look at you and think you're just walking slowly.

That you rest too long.

That you cancel plans too often.

That you need to "pull yourself together."

That you look completely fine.

But they're not inside your body.

They don't know how much energy the most ordinary things can take.

Don't know how many decisions you have to make every single day.

Don't know how many times you've planned something, only to have your condition force you to change it.

And sometimes the hardest part isn't the condition itself.

It's having to constantly explain it to other people.

Prove it.

Justify it.

Apologize for it.

Compare yourself to people who can do what you currently can't.

Please, stop.

You don't have to live on someone else's schedule.

Your body doesn't owe the world's expectations anything.

You're not obligated to be productive every day.

Not obligated to smile constantly.

Not obligated to turn every free moment into an achievement.

You have the right to rest without guilt.

The right to ask for help.

The right to use whatever support and tools are available to you.

The right to say, "I can't today."

That doesn't make you less valuable.

A person doesn't become less worthy because of their body's limits.

Your worth isn't measured in hours worked.

Not measured in speed.

Not measured in physical strength.

Not measured by how many tasks you got through.

You remain a person who deserves respect, love, friendship, joy, and a full life.

Sometimes the world makes you feel like you're the problem — because it wasn't built with your needs in mind.

But those are not the same thing.

If a staircase is inaccessible to someone in a wheelchair, that doesn't mean something is wrong with the person.

Sometimes the space needs to change.

The rules need to change.

The pace needs to change.

The expectations need to change.

And that's okay.

Please stop measuring yourself against someone else's ruler.

Your life might look different.

But "different" doesn't mean "worse."

You're allowed to build a life around whatever actually helps you feel better.

And if today your maximum was simply getting through the day, let that be enough.

You don't have to earn your right to exist through your productivity.

If this feels bigger than a website can hold, that's okay — that's what Support, below, is for.
