Loneliness

Invisible, even in a room full of people.

There's a particular kind of loneliness that can't be measured by how many people are around you.

You can sit in a room full of friends and feel completely like a stranger.

You can get messages and still be waiting for the one person you could actually write "I feel bad" to.

You can smile, joke, keep the conversation going, help other people — and then close your bedroom door and feel a huge emptiness.

And no one will know.

Sometimes the most painful part of loneliness isn't the absence of people.

It's the feeling that no one actually sees you.

Like everyone knows your name, knows what you do, sees your photos, hears your jokes — but no one knows how hard things get for you sometimes.

If that's exactly what you're feeling right now, please don't draw the conclusion:

"So I must not matter to anyone."

Loneliness has a very convincing voice, but it can't actually predict your future.

The fact that the right person isn't around right now doesn't mean they never will be.

The fact that you weren't understood before doesn't mean you're impossible to understand.

The fact that some people left doesn't mean everyone will.

And the fact that it's hard for you to open up right now doesn't mean you'll be alone forever.

Sometimes, after enough pain, a person starts protecting themselves.

They stop reaching out first.

Stop talking about their problems.

Stop asking for help.

They start thinking: "What's the point — no one's going to understand anyway."

But behind that protection, it gets quieter and quieter.

So try to take at least one small step toward the world.

You don't have to tell someone your whole story.

You could write:

"Hey. How are you?"

You could say:

"I'm having a hard time today, for some reason. Could you talk with me a bit?"

You could just sit next to someone.

Sometimes closeness doesn't start with a deep conversation.

It starts with a small presence.

And if you don't have anyone to write to right now, that doesn't mean something is wrong with you.

Sometimes life really does bring us into periods where very few people are around.

But a period is not a life sentence.

You deserve relationships where you don't have to constantly prove your worth.

You deserve people you can be quiet with.

You're allowed to be tired.

Allowed to be strange.

Allowed to not know what to say.

Allowed to not be fun.

Allowed to just be yourself.

And if you're reading this alone right now, let these few minutes at least be a reminder:

you don't have to pretend you're not lonely.

Your feeling is real.

Your need for closeness is normal.

Wanting to be heard doesn't make you weak.

And somewhere ahead of you, there may be conversations, people, whole relationships you don't even know exist yet.

Don't close the door for good just because a few people turned out to be the wrong ones.

Sometimes it just takes time to find the people you can finally stop pretending with.

If this feels bigger than a website can hold, that's okay — that's what Support, below, is for.
