Burnout

Hollowed out from giving too much, for too long.

Maybe you've spent too long being the person who copes.

You said:

"I'll manage."

"Just a little more."

"I'll finish and then rest."

"I can't let people down."

"I just need to push through."

And every time, you actually found the strength to keep going.

Until one day there was almost nothing left inside.

Now even small things feel irritating.

Messages feel like obligations.

Work feels heavy before you've even started.

People are exhausting.

Rest doesn't always help anymore.

You can spend a whole day doing nothing and still go to bed feeling like you've been unloading train cars all day.

And then the guilt shows up.

"I didn't do anything."

"I've gotten lazy."

"I used to be able to do so much more."

But maybe the problem isn't that you've gotten weaker.

Maybe you've been living at your limit for too long.

You can't keep giving out energy forever without ever letting yourself get any back.

You're not a machine.

You're not obligated to run at full capacity every single day.

And you don't have to destroy yourself completely to earn the right to stop.

Rest shouldn't be the last item on the list, after everything else is done.

Because the list never actually ends.

There's always one more message.

One more deadline.

One more request.

One more problem.

So sometimes you have to be the one who draws the line.

Not because you stopped caring.

But because you have a place in your own life too.

Try asking yourself today: "What can I not do?"

Not what to add.

Not how to be more productive.

Not how to fit more in.

But what you could take off your own shoulders.

You have the right to a pause.

The right to say no.

The right to take a day off.

The right to change your plans.

The right to admit, "I'm not managing the way I used to."

That's not admitting defeat.

That's honesty.

And being honest with yourself is sometimes exactly where recovery begins.

If this doesn't pass, is affecting your life significantly, or comes with real depression, anxiety, or thoughts of hurting yourself, please reach out to a professional.

You don't have to wait until you're completely broken.

You're allowed to ask for help earlier than that.

Please stop believing you're only worth something when you're useful.

You have value even when you're not producing anything.

Even lying down.

Even resting.

Even on a day when nothing got done.

You're still you.

And you're allowed to stop.

If this feels bigger than a website can hold, that's okay — that's what Support, below, is for.
